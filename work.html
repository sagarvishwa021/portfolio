02 — Email Copywriting

Cold outreach that reads like a person wrote it.

Reqx Technologies, a specialised talent acquisition and recruitment firm, was looking for an email copywriter for a cold email prospecting lead sequence.

As the email copywriter on the project, I worked with the client’s appointed team to drive clicks, opens and conversions as cold leads progressed through the email marketing lead sequence. The primary challenge was cutting through crowded B2B inboxes, overcoming low response rates from busy hiring managers, and striking the right balance between professional authority and human approachability.

Email marketing strategy

As the email marketing funnel was specifically created to convert cold leads, it was essential to avoid sounding like an automated sales blast.

The emails would be sent in plain-text emails (rather than heavily styled HTML templates) to ensure deliverability directly into the primary inbox rather than the promotions tab. Instead of pitching generic “recruitment services,” emails focused directly on immediate client headaches such as lengthy hiring cycles, talent drop-offs, and under-qualified applicant pools.

Copywriting strategy

To build trust without being aggressive, I structured cold sequences around a 3-part framework:

  1. The Opener A punchy, low-friction hook highlighting a specific industry hiring challenge and introducing a pre-vetted candidate profile or solution.
  2. The Social Proof Follow-Up A short case study or metric proving how Reqx solved the exact hiring problem for a similar client.
  3. The Soft CTA A zero-pressure closing line asking for interest rather than demanding a 30-minute sales call.

Endless optimisation

For each email, I drew on copywriting principles like storytelling and psychological triggers like a sense of urgency around their open roles, paired with immediate social proof to build trust. We used open and closed loops to keep the reader engaged and eagerly anticipating the next email.

Working with the client team, we continually optimised, edited and perfected the sequence as well as the subject lines, body copy and calls to action. We split tested variations to steadily increase engagement.

03 — Video Scriptwriting

A script is a performance, not a read.

When it comes to script writing, it’s a completely different ballgame. It’s less about a quiet read and more about a performance—demanding seamless coordination between visuals, voice delivery, and pacing.

Like any solid writing, it all starts with deep research. I begin by building a clear story outline. It’s like the architectural blueprint of how the narrative will unfold. Once the structure is locked, I focus on the hook. Crafting an effective opening takes real intention, so I usually leverage negative framing, curiosity gaps, or specific psychological triggers to stop the scroll.

From there, I fill in the narrative with only the information that actually serves the story, because cramming in every single piece of data just kills the momentum.

English translation

Something is being hidden from you on ‘Tamilnadu’. Meet Keeladi—a small village located near the Vaigai river.

In 2014, the Archaeological Survey of India began excavations here, and what they found shook the entire timeline of Indian history. They unearthed over 18,000 artifacts, advanced brick structures, and proper drainage systems. Essentially, a complete urban civilization!

Carbon dating revealed that the site dates back to the 8th to 6th Century BCE—making it roughly 2,800 years old. This discovery is massive because, until now, it was widely believed that such civilizations only existed in North India and later spread to the South.

But Keeladi proves that the Tamil civilization had its own parallel, independent identity. Yet, as soon as this report was released, the Central Government transferred the lead archaeologist of the Keeladi project.

The Tamil Nadu government alleges that the Centre is trying to downplay their heritage. Whatever the truth is, one thing is certain: Whoever controls the narrative of history, controls the power.

Blue Whale

English translation

If you think the blue whale is a fish, this video will completely change your mind.

While all fish lay eggs, blue whales cannot. They give birth to live young, feed them milk, and on top of that, blue whales can’t even breathe underwater!

So the question arises: if the blue whale is an animal just like us, what is it doing in the water?

Well, 50 million years ago, the blue whale’s ancestor—the Pakicetus—was actually a land-dwelling animal. It spent its life living along the coastlines or in shallow waters. At that time, its biggest struggle was finding food, as competition with other land animals was incredibly fierce.

Around the same time, the massive ocean predators of that era had already gone extinct. This meant the ocean was actually much safer than the land. Taking advantage of this, the Pakicetus started going into the sea to hunt for food.

Gradually, this marine environment suited it so well that, over time, its body evolved into a fish-like shape—ultimately becoming the blue whale we know today.

Super El Niño

English translation

Stay alert if you are stepping out in the sun! Because this level of heat is returning after 150 long years.

Renowned meteorologist Ryan Maue recently warned the world that this year’s El Niño could break the record set by the devastating 1877 El Niño. Just for context: when the 1877 El Niño hit, it wiped out roughly 4% of the entire human population.

To understand the El Niño effect, think of it like this: under normal conditions, trade winds blow from the Pacific Ocean toward Australia and the Indian subcontinent. This pushes warm ocean water toward our coast, causing evaporation, which eventually gives us a healthy, regular monsoon.

But during an El Niño year, this entire process flips in reverse. Instead of blowing toward us, the winds head toward South America, dragging all that warm ocean water along with them.

As a result, our side of the world is forced to face brutal heatwaves, severe droughts, and major monsoon disruptions.
